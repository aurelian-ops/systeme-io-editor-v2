# Conversion skill

Distilled from research/conversion-research.md (Unbounce 2024 benchmark of 41k pages, NN/g eyetracking, Baymard, CXL, Copyhackers, Schwartz, Hormozi, Brunson, StoryBrand). Each "## " part is sent only when it fits the request; parts starting with "Always" are always sent.

## Always: the rules that matter most

1. One page, one goal, one action. Every button points to the same action (repeating it is good, offering different ones is bad). Funnel pages have no navigation menu and no social links; the footer holds only legal links.
2. Clarity beats cleverness. In 5 seconds a visitor knows what this is, who it is for and what to do. No puns that need decoding.
3. The first screen (on a phone, about 390×750) carries the page: an outcome headline, a subheadline (who + how + how fast), the main button or the form, a one-line proof ("Joined by 2,341 students", "4.8/5 from 312 reviews"), and a visual of the product, the person or the result. Never a carousel there.
4. Write at a 5th–7th grade reading level: short words, sentences under 20 words, paragraphs of 1–3 sentences. (Pages at that level convert at 11.1% median vs 5.3% for college-level.)
5. Mobile first: 83% of visits are on phones. One column, buttons at least 48px tall and often full width, body text 16–18px.
6. Proof next to every big claim and every button after the first: a name, a face, a specific result with a number or timeframe.
7. Answer the objections (price, time, "will it work for me", trust, past failures, what exactly do I get, access) somewhere: in the copy, the FAQ, the guarantee or the line under the button.
8. Reverse the risk: a named, time-boxed guarantee next to the price on anything sold.
9. Specific beats general: "Lose 4.5 kg in 6 weeks with 20-minute home workouts", not "Get fit fast". Concrete nouns, numerals (7, not seven), timeframes.
10. Every section does one job (promise, proof, objection, offer, risk reversal or call to action). A section that does none of these is cut.

Voice:
- Write to one person ("you"; the creator says "I", a team says "we"). Buttons speak in the first person ("Get my free plan").
- The customer's words, not the creator's jargon ("fall asleep fast", not "optimise sleep architecture").
- Every feature gets its benefit: "30 done-for-you templates, so you can launch this weekend".
- Banned unless quoted from a real customer: revolutionary, game-changing, ultimate, unlock, unleash, skyrocket, supercharge, cutting-edge, world-class, seamless, synergy, elevate; "secret" more than once; "!!!"; sentences in capitals; em dashes.
- Match the tone to the niche: yoga warm and calm, finance precise and reassuring, business coaching direct and confident.

Honesty (hard rules; they are also the law: FTC 2024 fake-review rule, FTC dark patterns, EU/UK rules):
- Never present invented testimonials, names, ratings, student counts, results or logos as real. Use the proof in the brief when there is some. When there is none, you may write illustrative ones so the page looks finished, with natural names and roles ("Maya R., project manager"; never "Sample student" or "Client name"), but give every such quote, stat or logo strip the prop "sample": true (the editor flags them with a small Sample tag to replace before publishing), and say so in your reply.
- No countdown, deadline or "only N spots" unless the user gave a real date or capacity. No resetting timers.
- No fake "was $497" anchors: a value stack uses honest values, labelled "value".
- Declines are neutral ("No thanks, I'll continue without the templates"), never shaming. Add-ons are never pre-ticked. Recurring price and how to cancel sit next to the button.
- No income or health claims without context; no cures.

Buttons:
- 2–5 words, verb first, naming what they get: "Send me the free plan", "Save my seat", "Get instant access — $47". Never "Submit", "Click here", "Sign up", "Learn more".
- "Buy / Order / Pay" only at checkout; low-commitment wording before ("See what's inside", "Request a free copy").
- One line of reassurance under every main button (12–14px): "Instant access · No spam · 30-day guarantee".
- The same action keeps the same wording everywhere on the page.

## Index: page types and awareness (for planning)

Page types and their default length: opt-in (1–3 phone screens, 150–400 words), sales low ticket under $100 (4–8 screens), sales high ticket (10–25 screens, long is fine when every section earns its place), webinar registration (2–4 screens), thank-you (1–2 screens), checkout (one page), upsell / one-time offer (2–6 screens), book-a-call / application (5–10 screens).
Length follows price × unfamiliarity: free and familiar = short, expensive or a new idea = long.

Awareness decides the opening (Eugene Schwartz). When unknown: problem aware for opt-ins and webinars, solution/product aware for sales pages reached from email.
- Unaware: open with a story, a surprising fact or an identity hook. "Why 80% of new coaches quit in year one (it's not their skills)".
- Problem aware: open with the problem in their words (PAS). "Tired of posting every day and still getting no clients?"
- Solution aware: open with your unique mechanism. "The referral-first method: get clients without social media".
- Product aware: open with proof and the offer. "Join 1,200 coaches inside Client Engine — see their results".
- Most aware: open with the offer and price. "Client Engine closes Friday: $497 or 3 × $177".

Offer framing (Hormozi value equation): the page needs at least one element for each lever: dream outcome up (the headline names the end state), likelihood up (proof, credentials, guarantee, the mechanism), time down ("first result in 7 days", quick-start), effort down ("15 min a day", templates, done-for-you). Bonuses that each answer an objection beat discounts. Name offers with Avatar + Goal + Interval + Container: "The 30-Day Busy Mom Yoga Reset".

## Page: opt-in (lead magnet)

Goal: the email address. Order:
1. Hero: headline (specific outcome + the freebie's format), subheadline (who + time to value), a mock-up of the freebie, the form (email, or first name + email), the button, a privacy line.
2. "Inside you'll discover": 3–5 benefit bullets with curiosity.
3. Micro-proof: one testimonial or a subscriber count.
4. About the author: 2–3 lines and a photo.
5. Repeat the button (scrolls to the form).
6. Minimal footer.
Must: no navigation; the form in the first phone screen; the freebie shown; 1–2 fields; privacy reassurance. Mistakes: vague freebies ("Free guide"), long bios first, asking for a phone, "Submit".
Headlines: "Free 7-day plan: fall asleep in 15 minutes without pills" · "The 12-question script coaches use to close discovery calls". Buttons: "Send me the free plan", "Get my checklist".

## Page: sales (low ticket, under about $100)

Goal: an impulse purchase. Order:
1. Hero: outcome headline, product visual, button (with price when the audience already knows the product).
2. The problem in 3–5 lines, in their words.
3. The solution and what makes it different (the mechanism).
4. What's inside: modules or chapters as benefit bullets.
5. Proof: 3–6 testimonials.
6. Value stack and price ("Total value $214 — today $27", honest values only).
7. Bonuses, each answering an objection.
8. Guarantee: badge and specific wording.
9. Who it's for / not for.
10. FAQ (5–8 questions).
11. Final button with a recap of the offer.
Must: visible price, product visual, 3+ testimonials, guarantee next to the price, 3+ buttons, FAQ, "instant access" said.
Headline: "Plan a week of healthy dinners in 30 minutes — even if you hate cooking". Buttons: "Get instant access — $27", "Start the challenge for $17".

## Page: sales (high ticket, $500+: programs, signature courses, memberships)

Order (long-form, problem → offer → proof → close):
1. Hero: big-promise headline, subheadline with mechanism and audience, optional video; button "Join now" or "See plans" (scrolls to the offer).
2. Problem and agitation: their situation, the cost of staying stuck, what they tried.
3. The creator's story: struggle → discovery → result.
4. The mechanism: why old ways fail and this works.
5. Results wall: 6–12 specific testimonials, case studies.
6. The program: modules as outcomes, week by week.
7. Bonuses, each tied to an objection.
8. Value stack, price and a payment plan ("or 3 × $333").
9. Guarantee (conditional works well: "do the work, and if you don't get X, we refund").
10. Who it's for / not for.
11. About the creator: credentials and photo.
12. FAQ (8–12).
13. Final close: two futures ("six months from now…"), a real deadline only if one exists, the button.
14. Optional P.S. restating offer, guarantee and button.
Must: mechanism, 6+ specific testimonials, payment plan, guarantee, creator photo, FAQ, a button after every block from the offer on. Mistakes: leading with the bio, features without outcomes ("12 modules, 48 videos").
Headlines: "How desk workers fix back pain in 15 minutes a day — even if they've never done yoga" · "The 5-message sequence that books discovery calls". Buttons: "Join Client Engine", "Enroll now — start Monday".

## Page: webinar (masterclass registration)

Order:
1. Hero: label ("Free live masterclass"), headline (the big outcome), date, time and timezone (or "next session in 15 min"), host photo, register button.
2. "In this free training you'll learn": 3 lessons, each breaking a false belief, curious not salesy.
3. Who it's for.
4. Host: photo and 3 credibility facts.
5. Proof: past attendee or student results.
6. Repeat the button and the date.
7. Optional attendance bonus ("Stay to the end for the free workbook").
Must: date + time + timezone in the hero, host face, 3 learning bullets, 1–2 fields, "you'll get a reminder". Mistakes: missing timezone, fake "seats filling up".
Headlines: "Free masterclass: how to get your first 10 clients without ads — Thursday 7pm CET". Buttons: "Save my free seat", "Reserve my spot".

## Page: thank-you (confirmation)

Order: 1. Confirmation headline ("You're in! Check your inbox for the plan"). 2. The next 1–3 steps, numbered (open the email from [name], add to calendar, join the group). 3. One next action or one small related offer ($7–$27). 4. Optional welcome video (under 2 min).
Must: clear confirmation, where to find what they got, ONE next action; for webinars add-to-calendar and a reminder. Mistakes: a dead end, several competing buttons, an unrelated hard sell.

## Page: checkout (order form)

One page, two columns on desktop, stacked on phones:
1. Product summary: name, image, 3 bullets of what's included, price with billing frequency and taxes.
2. The form: email → name → payment. No account or password. Address only for physical goods. At most 7 fields.
3. Payment methods: card plus a wallet when available.
4. Order bump: a box with a checkbox (never pre-ticked), short headline, 1–2 sentence benefit, price.
5. Trust cluster at the payment fields: padlock, "Secure checkout", guarantee badge, payment logos.
6. One short testimonial.
7. Button with the amount: "Complete my order — $47".
8. Fine print: refund policy, terms, recurring terms in plain words.
Must: total visible before the card fields, no navigation away. Headline: "You're one step away from [outcome]".

## Page: upsell (one-time offer)

Order: 1. Status line ("Order complete ✓ — step 2 of 3, don't close this page"). 2. Bridging headline ("Your order is confirmed. Want [result] twice as fast?"). 3. Short video or 3–5 paragraphs: why this is the logical next step. 4. What you get: 3–5 bullets with value. 5. One-click button with the price ("Yes, add this to my order — $97"). 6. Guarantee line. 7. A plain, honest decline link. 8. Downsell if declined.
Must: relevant to the first product, price on the button, a clear decline.

## Page: book-a-call (coaching application)

Order: 1. Hero: outcome headline, who it's for ("For coaches doing $3k–10k a month"), button "Apply for a free strategy call". 2. The gap: where they are vs where they want to be. 3. How it works in 3 steps. 4. 3–5 case studies (before → what we did → after, with numbers). 5. About the coach. 6. What happens on the call ("30 min on Zoom, we map your next 90 days; if it's not a fit, I'll tell you"). 7. Who it's not for. 8. Multi-step application (5–8 questions, contact details last). 9. FAQ including the price range.
Headline: "Get [outcome] in 90 days with 1:1 coaching". Buttons: "Apply for a strategy call", "See if I qualify".

## Copy toolkit

Headlines (6–12 words, outcome in the first 3 words; check the 4 U's: useful, urgent, unique, ultra-specific):
- [Outcome] in [time] without [pain]: "Touch your toes in 30 days without painful stretching" · "Sign 3 new clients in 30 days without cold DMs" · "Pay off $10,000 of debt in 12 months without a second job".
- How [avatar] [outcome] — even if [objection]: "How freelancers build a 6-month safety net — even on irregular income".
- The [N]-[unit] [method] that [result]: "The 12-minute morning flow that ends stiff mornings".
- Finally, [desired state] for [avatar]: "Finally, a money plan for people who hate spreadsheets".
- Stop [pain]. Start [gain].: "Stop chasing leads. Start choosing clients."
- A customer quote: "'I finally sleep through the night' — 21-day bedtime yoga".
- A qualifying question: "Do you earn well but still feel broke?"
Subheadline: adds who + how (mechanism) + how fast/easy, 15–30 words, never repeats the headline. "A 21-day video program for beginners over 40: 15-minute daily flows that rebuild flexibility without strain."
Bullets (3–7, under 20 words, benefit + curiosity): "How to get your first client without posting daily on Instagram" · "5 budget leaks that drain $200 a month — #3 takes 2 minutes to fix" · "Why raising your price can make clients say yes faster".
Testimonials: a bold result line as the quote's headline, then 1–3 sentences (before → after → result → feeling), real face, name, role or place ("Mum of 2, Leeds"). Mix in one nuanced 4-star voice; a wall of perfect 5.0 looks fake.
Guarantee: name it, time-box it, make the process clear: "The 30-day 'love it or your money back' guarantee. Go through the first two modules. If you don't feel more confident on camera, email us within 30 days and we refund every cent. No forms, no hoops."
FAQ: written as the buyer asks; each answer starts with "Yes" or "No" plus one sentence. Order: price/value → "will it work for me" → time → access → refund → support. A button right after it.
Frameworks: PAS (problem-aware openings), AIDA (overall flow), BAB (short sections, testimonials, upsells), StoryBrand (the customer is the hero, the creator the guide with a 3-step plan), Epiphany Bridge (the creator's story).

## Checklist: scoring a page (100 points)

Each check passes (full weight), half passes (half) or fails (0). Skip checks that don't apply (12–15 on thank-you pages, 13 on opt-ins; on checkout the form is the call to action) and scale to 100. The top fixes are the failed checks with the highest weight × how badly they failed; on ties, the one higher on the page. Name the section and give the exact new copy.
1. Single goal (6): every button points to the same action; no navigation menu.
2. Outcome headline (7): the first headline names a result (not the brand, not "Welcome"), ideally with a number or timeframe.
3. Subheadline says who/how (4): 10–35 words under the headline, not a paraphrase.
4. Button or form in the first screen (6).
5. Specific button words (4): not Submit / Click here / Sign up / Learn more; 2–5 words, verb first.
6. Buttons repeated for the length (3): short pages 1–2; long pages one every ~2 phone screens after the offer, and one at the end.
7. Reassurance under the main button (3).
8. Proof in the first screen (4): rating, count, logos or a short quote.
9. Specific testimonials (6): name and photo, half with a number or timeframe; opt-in 1+, low-ticket sales 3+, high-ticket 6+.
10. Proof next to the repeated buttons (3).
11. Benefits as outcomes (4): most bullets say "so you can / how to / without / in X".
12. Offer clarity (5): what's included, the price and the format/access are stated; opt-ins name and show the freebie.
13. Value framing (3): honest value stack or comparison; a payment plan above $300.
14. Risk reversal (5): a guarantee near the price; a privacy line on opt-ins.
15. Objections / FAQ (4): 5+ questions covering price, time, fit, access, refund.
16. Form friction (6): opt-in ≤ 2 fields, registration ≤ 3, digital checkout ≤ 7 with no password; visible labels.
17. Readability (5): grade 7 or lower, sentences ≤ 20 words, no hype words.
18. Scannability (3): every section has a meaningful heading; no text block over 80 words; lists for 3+ items.
19. Message match (4): when the traffic source is known, the headline repeats its key words.
20. Visual of the product or person (3) in the hero, not generic stock.
21. Mobile (4): one column, buttons ≥ 44px tall, body ≥ 16px, form in the first screen on opt-ins.
22. Speed (3): light hero image, at most one video, no autoplay background video.
23. Accessibility (3): contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (button text too), image alt text, one H1, labelled inputs.
24. Trust and disclosure (3): legal footer, the creator named, total and recurring terms at checkout.
25. Honest urgency only (3, and −10 extra if failed): any countdown or scarcity has a real date or capacity.
Bands: 85–100 ready to launch · 70–84 good, fix the top 3 · 50–69 major gaps (usually proof, offer clarity, form friction) · under 50 rebuild with the page-type order.
